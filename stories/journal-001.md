Caltren, barely twenty, gripped his spear like a drowning man clutching driftwood. The top of Cindros' walls were slick with rain drizzle under a moody sky, sunlight meekly trying to make itself known. The scent of wet stone and oiled steel hung heavy in the air. He could practically feel the presence of the massive catapults flanking his battalion on either side like massive beasts of momentum. But today, it did nothing to still the ice in Caltren's veins.

The others stood beside him, shoulder to shoulder, row upon row, battalion upon battalion of Celian troops in dark sapphire livery, cloaks weighed down by the misting rain. Veterans with salt in their beards and emptiness in their eyes. Mages in pale robes, fingers twitching. Above them, mounted upon a few towers, were even a handful of Winged Concord. Armored riders hunched atop their wyverns, the savage looking lizards of saddle, scale, and precise savagery scanning the horizon.

Caltren felt like an imposter standing amongst them, attempting to swallow the anxiety lodged in his throat. Skirmishes, yes. But now he was about to witness the *thing* that has been the nightmare burning their borders. It had been ephemeral upon until now, and it was about to become all too real.

From the highest rampart of the eastward wall, he looked down upon the river valley that once shimmered silver-blue with mineral-rich waters. Now, it was dull, thick with overcast gloom.

And there, drawn across the horizon like some painter's blasphemous magnum-opus, came the gold.

The Saintillac.

They moved without noise. No trumpets. No fanfare. Just gold.

A seething, awful gold. It was like judgement rendered. The entire army shimmered with it. Row upon row of knights, helmed and sealed in flawless golden plate, their visors dark and expressionless. They moved with inhuman synchronicity. No idle chatter. No flagging lines. No hesitation. Held high upon obsidian scaffolds fluttered the immaculate banners of Our Lady of Gold.

What made Caltren almost puke were the machines they had brought with them. The monsters clearly had sent *everything* with their soldiers.

Tower crawlers with golden legs like titanic beetles. Throwers the size of ships. Siege engines with enormous hammers at their front, suspended by jointed arms of coiled sinew-steel. Armor plates laid over black-steel foundations. Flaring golden panels protecting every surface. Baroque spirals adorning every corner. Candles burning from mounted sconces. Symbolic archways embedded into sides. Rams shaped like inverted chapel spires. Deployable stakes, grappling claws, underbellies glowing with molten runes.

And then, circling high above the entire force were the **angels**.

Twelve of them hovered high, wings splayed like golden blades. Their bodies were tall and unnatural, sculpted from unfeeling porcelain. Their heads bore no features, just polished to a blind. Some of them dragged golden chains that hung like pendulums, others wielded blades of searing light.

This force that could level mountains reverberated through the valley with every step. *BOOM*.....*BOOM*.....*BOOM*.....

And it all sent one, unmistakable message.

*Your world is ending. And we are not sorry.*

Caltren's knees trembled beneath him, and he found himself gripping the stone of the wall for balance. His breath came quick and sharp. The spear in his hands felt like a toy. The inevitability of surrender made him want to sob. The Celian command barked orders behind him. Riders adjusted harnesses. Spellcasters drew wards. Wyverns growled and shifted nervously. Blades were pulled from sheathes.

Caltren couldn't breathe.

"Lad," said a voice to his right, deeper, grounded. Commander Hirum, broad as a wagon and twice as scarred. "Don't stare at them so long. That's what they want."

Caltren swallowed. The Saintillian army stretched across the entire valleyside like a cancer. Campfire lit in precise grids. Watch banners snapped in perfect unison. Thousands and *thousands* of them. Disciplined. Doctrine. Dread.

"I don't want to die," Caltren whispered weakly. He hadn't meant to say it out loud.

"No one here does, lad. But we're here anyway." He clapped a firm hand to Caltren's shoulder. "And if they want our city-they'll have to burn through the blood of everyone on these walls to take it."

The eerie silence of the golden army below stretched into infinity.

And the rain kept falling.
